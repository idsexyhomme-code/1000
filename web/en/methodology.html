How WorkRadar scores AI job risk

Short version: WorkRadar is a directional reference, not a prediction. It estimates how exposed a job's day-to-day tasks are to today's AI — and it's transparent about the fact that these are hand-built estimates, not the output of a trained model.

1. We score tasks, not job titles

"Will AI replace X?" is the wrong unit. A job is a bundle of tasks, and AI hits some tasks hard while barely touching others. So for each role we break the work into concrete tasks and estimate an AI-pressure (0–100) for each: how well can current, widely-deployed AI tools already do this specific task?

The role's headline number is a weighting of those task scores. That's why every job page shows the task breakdown — the number only means something with the tasks behind it.

2. What the score means (weather bands)

We map the 0–100 AI-pressure onto four bands, like a weather forecast for a role:

0–24 · Clear Little of the work is currently automatable.
25–44 · Partly cloudy Some tasks exposed; core of the role holds.
45–64 · Cloudy A meaningful share of tasks is under pressure.
65–100 · Storm Most day-to-day tasks are highly exposed.

High pressure means task reshuffling — routine slices shrink, judgment slices become the job — not that the role disappears overnight.

3. Where the estimates come from

  • Public AI signals. Task scores are informed by what shipping AI products and public announcements can demonstrably do, weighted toward real adoption rather than demos.
  • Data anchors. Where possible we anchor a role against published AI-exposure research — the AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index from Felten, Raj & Seamans (2021), "Occupational, industry, and geographic exposure to artificial intelligence" (Strategic Management Journal). We store only the percentile position, and label anchored roles so you can see which numbers have external grounding and which are our own estimate.

4. What this is not — the honest part

Scores are hand-estimated and uncalibrated. They are not the output of a validated statistical model, and they are not probabilities. Treat them as a structured opinion for orientation, not a forecast.
  • Not a prediction of whether any specific person keeps their job.
  • Not a verdict on any company, and not career or financial advice with guaranteed outcomes.
  • Rankings are relative to the set of roles we track — a #1 means "most exposed among these," not "certain to be automated."

5. Why we do it this way

Most "AI will take N jobs" content is fear-bait with no method shown. We'd rather publish a transparent, falsifiable frame — task-level, banded, source-anchored where we can — and be upfront about its limits. If a number looks wrong to you, the task breakdown tells you exactly which assumption to argue with.
